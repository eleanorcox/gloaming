# Vision

The working name is **Gloaming**, an old word, mostly Scottish, for dusk.

## In one sentence

A field of fireflies at dusk: many small lights drifting, flashing and gathering, responding to sound and to the people around them.

## Where it comes from

- **The aquarium's field of fish.** The aquarium has a scene of 700 fish streaming past in one school, filling the screen so you are in the middle of it. Gloaming is a cousin of that: many small creatures, simple rules, a mass that feels alive.
- **Sound.** Fireflies that respond to music or to a room's noise.
- **Projection mapping.** Fireflies are almost nothing but light on darkness, so they suit projection: on a wall, a ceiling, or among real plants. **Projection mapping** means shaping a projected picture to fit real surfaces and objects, instead of a flat rectangle on a screen.

## A British twist (unchecked)

Britain has very few fireflies in the sense of flying, flashing beetles. Its glowing insect is the **glow-worm**, *Lampyris noctiluca*: the females glow steadily from grass and hedgerows on summer nights to attract the males, which fly. That could shape the piece: steady glows low in the grass, with dim flying lights searching above them. It would also sit naturally beside [Cat's Eyes](../../cats-eyes/docs/vision.md), the night drive through the countryside.
